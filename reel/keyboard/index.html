The keyboard: an Apple replica with one difference

review prototype

Type on it. Hold a vowel. Double-tap shift. Hold the space bar. Hold 123 and slide onto a symbol. Tap the face beside 123 for a note about where it leads now. Everything here is meant to be indistinguishable from the iPhone keyboard except the small mark at the top left, which is ours. Tap it and your things come up over the keys. Turn on X-ray to see which parts are whose.

The row of field chips changes what the keyboard is asked for. A field tells the keyboard what it is for, and the keyboard answers. Email brings the at sign and the full stop onto the bottom row. Address brings the full stop, the slash and a .com key, and gives up the emoji key to fit them on a 393pt phone; hold .com for the other endings. Number and Decimal put a three-column pad there instead, with no letters at all. The keyboard is exactly the same height in every one of them.

The row beside the mark. Things you saved lead it from the third letter that starts a word inside one, anchored to the left edge; word suggestions fill the cells they leave, floating centred, from the second letter of any word. Three cells, and it stays empty when neither has anything to say, which is what your phone's own row does too. Press Play a line to watch, or type resu, head or omw yourself. Two keyboards, both current: Apple ships the flat one and the glass one at the same time and picks between them per app, so this is not a question of which year it is. The page opens on the flat one because that is what your capture shows.
